# First varied reef-fish pack — FAF-FISH-001–005

**Status, 2026-11-10:** Five original fish are registered as a first technical reef-fish library. FAF-FISH-001 Yellow Tang is preserved from its [existing candidate record](../fish-001/README.md). FAF-FISH-002–005 are deterministic, source-controlled builds with reviewed Blender silhouettes and deformation, separate GLBs, and individual isolated Godot Forward+ proofs. Reef-context and gameplay-camera style approval remain open. The current Fish player and movement are unchanged.

The approved [reef style target](../references/reef-style-target.png), [FAF art style](../art-style.md), [modeling](../modeling-standards.md), [rigging](../rigging-standards.md), [animation](../animation-standards.md), [budgets](../budgets.md), and [Blender–Godot handoff](../blender-godot-contract.md) governed the pack. Species shape/marking references: [Ocellaris clownfish](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/species-profiles/clown-anemonefish/), [Blue-Green Chromis](https://eprints.cmfri.org.in/14853/), [reef butterflyfish](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/species-profiles/reef-butterflyfish/), and [Blacktip Reef Shark](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/species-profiles/blacktip-reef-shark/). These inform anatomy, not copied meshes or textures.

## Candidate inventory

Dimensions are Blender source bounds in metres, X × Y × Z. Blender forward is −Y and up is +Z; Godot imports the forward axis as +Z, so the isolated proof fixture rotates the instance 180° around Y for scene-facing −Z. All new object transforms are location/rotation zero and scale one, with origin at the body pivot. All new fish use one armature, three opaque materials, and 24 FPS authored Actions.

## Source, reuse, and rig differences

The four new candidates live in one [editable Blender master](../../../assets/faf/fish/reef_fish_002_to_005.blend), built by [`fish.build_reef_pack_001`](../../../tools/asset-factory/blender/fish/build_reef_pack_001.py) from the source-controlled [`fish.reef_families`](../../../tools/asset-factory/blender/fish/reef_families.py) blueprints. Each blueprint carries its own loft stations, cross-section density, fin construction, colors, bone chain, and wave profile. Shared construction handles the loft, simple fin solids, FAF bone roles, deterministic station weights, and three Actions. The shark has a separate fin recipe and eight body-chain bones versus five or six on the small fish; its forebody has much lower wave amplitude and its tail chain has a slower, stronger sweep. Tang geometry remains in its original source and was not rebuilt.

New exported hierarchy: `root → spine_* → tail_* → tail_fin`, with left/right pectorals, dorsal and anal branches. Eyes are rigidly bound to `spine_01`; body weights blend along the length, and fins are attached to their named control/deform bone. The Chromis omits `spine_04`; the shark adds `spine_05` and `tail_03`. Glide has 30% of each species' base wave amplitude, Swim uses the base profile, and SwimFast uses 135% at a shorter cycle. All three Actions have an exact first/last Blender pose seam and no translation tracks. The common GLB exporter gained per-armature `NLA_TRACKS` mode because the initial multi-scene export had incorrectly included all open fish Actions; the final four GLBs contain exactly three species-owned Actions each.

The four GLBs are [Clownfish](../../../game/assets/faf/fish/fish_002_ocellaris_clownfish.glb), [Chromis](../../../game/assets/faf/fish/fish_003_blue_green_chromis.glb), [Butterflyfish](../../../game/assets/faf/fish/fish_004_reef_butterflyfish.glb), and [Blacktip Reef Shark](../../../game/assets/faf/fish/fish_005_blacktip_reef_shark.glb). Existing Tang source and GLB were not overwritten.

## Visual review and Godot proof

Blender MCP review covered each side, three-quarter, and strong SwimFast top pose. The compact clown silhouette retains three broad white bars with dark edges and a rounded fan tail; the Chromis keeps a small green/blue body and deep fork; the Butterflyfish has a thin tall body, snout, eye/rear bands, and broad yellow fins; the shark has a gray/pale body, distinct pectorals, asymmetric caudal, and black fin tips. Eyes and heads remained stable, body waves concentrated rearward, and no visible fin-root gaps or tail-base collapse appeared in the reviewed poses. The shark was also viewed in the opposite strong-tail direction. The initial clown tail was too forked and its fin polygon was corrected; the shark's initial snout was too needle-like and its loft stations were corrected before export.

## Remaining weaknesses and decision

The deliberately planar fin polygons read as hard shapes at close range, especially the Butterflyfish dorsal/anal and the shark pectorals. The Chromis' cyan-green body has limited contrast against bright blue water. The shark's black tip treatment is readable but simplified, and its size is a candidate scale rather than a gameplay-approved one. No fish has yet been reviewed swimming among approved rock/coral/plant/terrain pieces from the actual gameplay camera, so final palette balance, distance legibility, density and native reef-context style approval are pending. These five fish are ready as the **first technical reference pack and future-candidate starting point**, with their individual species not locking the eventual player species.
