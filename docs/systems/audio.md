# Runtime audio — Pond first pass

**Implemented for the Pike Encounter on 2026-11-14. The owner reviewed the initial mix; revised cue balance awaits another listen.** This is a presentation system. It reads the fish motor and growth state after each physics tick and never writes velocity, collision, feeding or AI state. Normal Ocean and Pond play do not yet have a runtime bed; the Pond track is installed only in Pike Encounter, so it cannot play in Ocean.

## Signal path and tuning

`WorldAudio` is one scene-scoped node on Pike Encounter. Its `AudioStreamPlayer` under `WorldAudio` plays the stereo bed once through the `Ambience` bus. The fish owns reusable `AudioStreamPlayer3D` emitters for swim and boost events; their bus is `SFX`. A new emitter is added only when every existing emitter for that cue is still playing, so overlapping cues keep independent pitch and volume until each take finishes. The bus layout sets Master at -3 dB, Ambience at -2 dB and SFX at -1 dB. The active Godot camera is the spatial listener, including the egg intro camera until the fry camera becomes current. No extra listener is installed. Emitters use 2 m unit size, 14 m maximum distance and -8 dB maximum gain. The shared `WorldAudio.CreateEmitter` API can attach later positional sounds to other actors.

Depth is `max(0, configured habitat water surface Y - fish global Y)`. Pike Encounter obtains the surface from the loaded Pond `SeabedSurface` via `FishController.PondWaterlineHeight`. Camera position cannot change this sample. The initial target is -39 dB at the surface, rising continuously after 0.5 m to -14 dB by 9 m. A smoothstep response raised to power 1.25 shapes the rise. Current gain approaches target exponentially at 1.5 per second, about a 0.67-second time constant. Exported parameters on `WorldAudio` allow adjustment of both gains, rise/full depths, response power and fade speed. Surface breaches clamp to the surface target; very deep samples clamp to the deep target. Depth updates change only the bed's `VolumeDb`, never call `Play`. The owner accepted the ambient water sound and its depth response unchanged.

A normal swim onset cue plays once the fish is actually moving under effort, after reaching 12% of its normal maximum speed. It re-arms after 0.2 seconds near rest. Steering cues read the fish's measured yaw and pitch changes after the motor's collision-safe turn decision. A blocked turn or a click without movement is silent. A turn cue requires the fish's actual speed to be at least 40% of the current growth stage's normal maximum speed. Below that speed, turn activity resets and no new turn cue plays; a cue already playing finishes normally. The measured turn rate is smoothed over about 0.12 seconds; it must also reach 40% of the maximum angular rate before a cue plays. This filters light mouse movements and isolated one-frame motion. Sustained turning renews a cue no sooner than 0.65–0.9 seconds, depending on strength. Each turning cue selects a fresh length within a strength-dependent range: light qualifying turns use about 44–64% of the source, and full-strength turns use about 74–94%. Adjacent turn lengths differ by at least 8% of the source. Ordinary swim and boost cues still vary between 72% and 98% independently. A transition into the FishController's actual `BoostActive` state emits one boost cue and takes priority on that tick. Input re-arm, pause, focus loss and scene departure reset the cue gate. Larva uses the gentle or boost sound at very low gain; juvenile is louder; subadult/adult normal swim uses the heavier take, and boost uses that take with a lower base pitch and stronger gain. Each cue varies its pitch within 16% of its stage base, with adjacent pitches separated by at least about 5%. Turning cues also vary by up to 3 dB around the stage gain, with at least 1.5 dB between consecutive turns. Each cue's selected length accounts for its pitch and fades during its final 0.12 seconds (or less for a very short cue). Idle emitters are reused; playing emitters are never stopped to make room for a new cue. After the owner's first listen, eating cues were lowered 6 dB (small food -24 dB, fish -18 dB), while every normal swim and boost stage gain was raised 3 dB. Current normal swim gains are larva -32 dB, juvenile -17 dB, subadult -16 dB and adult -13 dB; boost gains are -26, -15, -13 and -11 dB respectively. Swim and boost source gains do not depend on fish depth; their 3D distance attenuation still follows the active camera and fish positions.

The World Select scene has no world audio node. Retry and World Select scene changes dispose the old scene's bed and effects. Unrelated immediate-exit headless gameplay checks omit the presentation node; the dedicated `inspect-audio` check exercises it and waits for audio-thread shutdown before exit.

## Qualification boundary

`./tools/dev import`, `./tools/dev build`, `./tools/dev inspect-audio`, `./tools/dev inspect-hatch`, and `./tools/dev inspect-auto-feed-boost` check imports, C# compilation, curve monotonicity/smoothing, one bed, bus routing, emitter ownership, movement and measured-turn gating, pitch/turn-volume/length bounds, independently faded overlapping cues, audio-scene retry/World Select cleanup, hatch/retry and boost integration. Godot's headless checks cannot prove seamless perceived looping, natural panning, loudness balance or speaker/headphone impression. The owner should audition the running Pike Encounter at surface, intermediate and deep depths, then try gentle and strong turns, a blocked turn, a valid boost, growth, pause, retry and return to World Select. No Roadmap Gate is advanced by these checks.
