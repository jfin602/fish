**Implemented for the Pike Encounter on 2026-11-14, with surface splash, shallow Pond ambience and environmental bubble accents added on 2026-11-15. The owner reviewed the initial deep-bed mix; the revised cues, splash, shallow/deep balance and bubble accents await another listen.** This is a presentation system. ## Signal path and tuning

`WorldAudio` is one scene-scoped node on Pike Encounter. Two non-positional `AudioStreamPlayer` children each start once through the `Ambience` bus: the original deep bed and the new shallow bed. The fish owns reusable `AudioStreamPlayer3D` emitters for swim, boost and splash events; registered Bluegill actors own splash emitters. Their bus is `SFX`. A new emitter is added only when every existing emitter for that cue is still playing, up to eight swim/boost voices or four Bass splash voices (three per Bluegill splash type). Overlapping cues keep independent pitch and volume until each take finishes. The bus layout sets Master at -3 dB, Ambience at -2 dB and SFX at -1 dB. The active Godot camera is the spatial listener, including the egg intro camera until the fry camera becomes current. No extra listener is installed. Emitters use 2 m unit size, 14 m maximum distance and -8 dB maximum gain.

## Shared one-shot variation and surface events

`SfxVariationProfile` declares pitch multiplier bounds and adjacent separation, dB bounds and separation, duration fraction bounds and separation, optional intensity gains and duration ranges, and fade time. `SfxVariationState.Next(profile, intensity)` owns the independent random history for one cue category; an optional seed and `Sample` with explicit samples support deterministic tests. `SfxOneShotPlayer.Play(stream, profile, intensity, worldPosition)` handles bounded 3D voices, source-duration conversion after pitch scaling, early-stop fade, voice reuse and cancellation. `WorldAudio` uses separate players for swim onset, turning, boost, surface arrival and rapid dive. Existing swim/boost pitch remains ±16% with at least 5% adjacent separation; turn gain remains ±3 dB with at least 1.5 dB adjacent separation. Ordinary cue length remains 72–98%; light/full turns span 44–64% / 74–94%, with 8% adjacent separation. Stage gains, movement gates and ambience response are unchanged.

`SurfaceSplashDetector` reads completed body position each physics tick against `FishController.PondSurfaceHeightAt(x, z)`. A contact occurs when center depth reaches 0.65 of the current body radius while actual measured upward speed is at least `max(0.035 m/s, 0.12 × stage maximum speed)`. The arrival rearms after 0.12 seconds at depth at least `max(0.012 m, 1.5 × radius)`. A rapid dive requires recent contact (within 0.22 seconds), increasing depth and measured downward speed at least `max(0.07 m/s, 0.32 × stage maximum speed)`. A 0.24-second cooldown and per-contact dive latch prevent chatter. Presentation observation uses the moving surface; the wake's wider activation band is not treated as contact. Ineligible play, hatch lock, focus loss, pause, retry and exit reset event state.

The registered Bass radii measured by `inspect-audio` are 0.0110, 0.0594, 0.1436 and 0.2232 m for larva through adult. Their corresponding contact depths are 0.0072, 0.0386, 0.0933 and 0.1451 m; rearm depths are 0.0165, 0.0891, 0.2154 and 0.3349 m. These values are relative to the sampled moving surface, not the historical static waterline.

Both events play the approved splash at the sampled water surface location. Exported `WorldAudio` controls start at arrival -20 dB, dive -18 dB, size reduction up to 9 dB for small fish, force gain up to 3 dB, pitch ±14%, duration 68–98% (the strongest events raise its lower bound to 74%), gain jitter ±2.5 dB, at least 4.5% adjacent pitch separation, 1 dB gain separation, 7% duration separation and a 0.10-second early-stop fade. These are initial listening values. Pike Encounter registers its Bluegill actors after their own `MoveAndSlide` ticks; their local emitters use the same detector and variation logic. The Pike actor deliberately patrols at least 1 m below the static water level and is not wired for surface splashes. Other Pond scenes have not installed this presentation system. The owner's native listen is still required before calling the mix qualified.

Depth is `max(0, configured habitat water surface Y - fish global Y)`. Pike Encounter obtains the surface from the loaded Pond `SeabedSurface` via `FishController.PondWaterlineHeight`. Camera position cannot change this sample. The initial target is -39 dB at the surface, rising continuously after 0.5 m to -14 dB by 9 m. A smoothstep response raised to power 1.25 shapes the rise. Current gain approaches target exponentially at 1.5 per second, about a 0.67-second time constant. Exported parameters on `WorldAudio` allow adjustment of both gains, rise/full depths, response power and fade speed. Surface breaches clamp to the surface target; very deep samples clamp to the deep target. Depth updates change only the bed's `VolumeDb`, never call `Play`. The owner accepted the ambient water sound and its depth response unchanged.

The shallow layer uses the fish's Y position relative to `FishController.PondSurfaceHeightAt(x, z)` at the fish's current X/Z, so it follows the moving Pond surface independently of the camera. Its surface target is **-20 dB**, raised from -26 dB after owner feedback for approximately twice the signal level. A smoothstep over 0–5 m lowers the target monotonically to -80 dB at 5 m and deeper; at 1 m the target is about -26.2 dB, and at 2.5 m it is -50 dB. The current gain approaches its target exponentially at 2 per second (about a 0.5-second time constant). `ShallowSurfaceVolumeDb`, `ShallowFadeDepth` and `ShallowFadeSpeed` are separate exported controls; changes affect only the shallow player. Depth updates set its `VolumeDb` without restarting the loop. Scene pause holds both players; focus loss pauses both streams; retry and scene exit stop and release both. The revised combined balance still needs owner listening, especially the shallow/deep transition and loop impression.

Pike Encounter also has one positional environmental bubble emitter on `SFX`. It loads only the three approved mono derivatives through `res://assets/sfx/` and chooses a different take from the previous event. A randomized 60-second active-underwater window schedules 1–4 events; gaps are at least 10 seconds, including across window boundaries, with randomized remaining space so there is no fixed rhythm. Time advances only while the player is actively controlling a fully submerged fish; the moving surface must be at least 0.05 m above the top of its collision radius. Each event samples a direction and 3–9 m radius around the fish, rejects positions outside the Pond interior or with insufficient water above the generated seabed, and places the voice at least 0.35 m above that floor and below the local moving surface. The initial emitter gain is **-8 dB**, with 2 m unit size, 12 m maximum distance and -8 dB maximum gain; the SFX and Master buses add -1 and -3 dB. Surface exit, pause, focus loss, retry and scene exit stop the current accent and reset its schedule, so time away cannot create queued bursts. This layer does not read visible bubbles, player actions or predator state, and it does not change either ambience bed or simulation state.

A normal swim onset cue plays once the fish is actually moving under effort, after reaching 12% of its normal maximum speed. It re-arms after 0.2 seconds near rest. Steering cues read the fish's measured yaw and pitch changes after the motor's collision-safe turn decision. A blocked turn or a click without movement is silent. A turn cue requires the fish's actual speed to be at least 40% of the current growth stage's normal maximum speed. Below that speed, turn activity resets and no new turn cue plays; a cue already playing finishes normally. The measured turn rate is smoothed over about 0.12 seconds; it must also reach 40% of the maximum angular rate before a cue plays. This filters light mouse movements and isolated one-frame motion. Sustained turning renews a cue no sooner than 0.65–0.9 seconds, depending on strength. Each turning cue selects a fresh length within a strength-dependent range: light qualifying turns use about 44–64% of the source, and full-strength turns use about 74–94%. Adjacent turn lengths differ by at least 8% of the source. Ordinary swim and boost cues still vary between 72% and 98% independently. A transition into the FishController's actual `BoostActive` state emits one boost cue and takes priority on that tick. Input re-arm, pause, focus loss and scene departure reset the cue gate. Larva uses the gentle or boost sound at very low gain; juvenile is louder; subadult/adult normal swim uses the heavier take, and boost uses that take with a lower base pitch and stronger gain. Each cue varies its pitch within 16% of its stage base, with adjacent pitches separated by at least about 5%. Turning cues also vary by up to 3 dB around the stage gain, with at least 1.5 dB between consecutive turns. Each cue's selected length accounts for its pitch and fades during its final 0.12 seconds (or less for a very short cue). Idle emitters are reused; playing emitters are never stopped to make room for a new cue. After the owner's first listen, eating cues were lowered 6 dB (small food -24 dB, fish -18 dB), while every normal swim and boost stage gain was raised 3 dB. Current normal swim gains are larva -32 dB, juvenile -17 dB, subadult -16 dB and adult -13 dB; boost gains are -26, -15, -13 and -11 dB respectively. Swim and boost source gains do not depend on fish depth; their 3D distance attenuation still follows the active camera and fish positions.

The World Select scene has no world audio node. Retry and World Select scene changes dispose the old scene's bed and effects. Unrelated immediate-exit headless gameplay checks omit the presentation node; the dedicated `inspect-audio` check exercises it and waits for audio-thread shutdown before exit.
