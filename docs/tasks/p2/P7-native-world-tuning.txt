A — V18 HABITAT / TRAVERSAL
1. Record revision/dirty identity, OS/display backend, selected renderer, Godot/.NET, display mode/resolution and player/camera/tuning identity.
2. Launch the normal P2 world and spend several minutes swimming rather than inspecting a still scene. Traverse one broad route, the intended rock tunnel/tight passage, the overhang/sheltered pocket and a lower-to-upper vertical route.
3. Verify the normal world no longer exposes colored P1 target spheres, camera-check cylinder, obvious flat double-ground/test-room boundaries or a second swimmable layer beneath the seabed. Probe edges/underside naturally and confirm the fish stays inside the intended world.
4. Judge rock/coral placement, repetition, seabed variation, sightlines, collision fairness and camera behavior. Confirm the normal-play world actually uses the intended prepared palette: Coast Sand 01, Ground060, Gravel041, Rock063; Rock Face 01, Rock Face 02, Coastal Cliff 01, Boulder 01; Heliopora coerulea, Acropora cervicornis, Acropora palmata, Meandrina meandrites and Montipora danae. Every listed model must be visibly represented and every listed terrain material must visibly contribute to the habitat. A missing prepared asset is a P4/P7 defect to repair, not a performance exception. Tune density, LOD/derived resources, geometry/collision and placement within P2 scope; do not change accepted motor semantics just to fit a bad tunnel. Preserve the P1 regression fixture separately.

B — V19 UNDERWATER ATMOSPHERE
1. Observe near/far and upper/lower/deeper viewpoints while moving. Confirm depth/distance treatment, underwater color/contrast, top light, moving caustic-style illumination and suspended matter work together so the scene reads clearly as submerged.
2. Observe the screen treatment through yaw, pitch, fast turns and slow glide. Reduce nausea/noise/distortion if needed. Fish, tunnel entrances and collision-relevant shapes must remain readable.
3. Open pause/settings and switch fullscreen/windowed-fullscreen as supported. UI text/focus must remain crisp and not be distorted like world geometry. Exercise focus loss/refocus for visual/input cleanup.
4. Compare the final renderer behavior to `qualification/renderer-decision.md`; if the full habitat materially invalidates that decision, stop and repair/re-evaluate rather than silently switching.
5. Record representative frame timing/FPS/performance counters in at least open water and the densest reef/effect view on the observed machine. The dense view should exercise the real mixed rock/coral/material palette, including at least one of the heavier Smithsonian/Poly Haven assets, so performance evidence covers the prepared content rather than placeholder geometry. Tune instance density, LOD/derived resources and bounded P2 effects for obvious regressions while retaining at least one visible use of every prepared model and visible use of every terrain material; do not invent a production budget.

C — V20 FISH-REACTIVE WATER
1. From near-rest/glide, ordinary cruise, acceleration/high effort and hard left/right turns, observe the wake/turbulence/bubble/trail behavior. It should scale distinctly and originate believably from the body/tail.
2. Climb/dive and turn near geometry; the effect should follow fish orientation, remain behind/around it and not obscure the camera/tunnel.
3. Pause, focus away/back, resume and restart. Verify no giant queued burst, stale emitter, stuck particles or movement change.
4. Tune intensity, lifetime, spread, count and turn response until the effect improves the sense of movement without becoming constant visual clutter.
