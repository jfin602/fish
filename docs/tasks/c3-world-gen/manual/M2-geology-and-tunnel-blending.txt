IMPLEMENT

A — SELECT GEOLOGY SITES FROM TERRAIN

1. Remove the current hard-coded geology coordinates/specs as the normal production strategy.
2. Analyze the finished M1 terrain for candidate geological sites:
   - thick ridges;
   - steep banks;
   - elevated shoulders;
   - rock-dominant slopes;
   - terrain masses with sufficient width/depth to contain a passage;
   - transitions that connect useful depth bands.
3. Score candidate sites for:
   - available terrain mass;
   - slope/ridge geometry;
   - relationship to shallow/middle/deep areas;
   - future route usefulness;
   - distance from shoreline fail-safe;
   - enough surrounding surface area to visually bury the structure.
4. Choose deterministic sites from those candidates per seed.
5. If a requested geology type has no credible site, generate/modify a suitable macro ridge before creating the tunnel rather than placing a floating shell in open water.

B — GENERATE LESS-GEOMETRIC FUNCTIONAL PASSAGES

6. Keep generated cave/tunnel geometry as actual floor/walls/ceiling/collision.
7. Stop using visually clean circular/oval tube cross-sections as the dominant visible shape.
8. Vary along the centerline:
   - width;
   - roof height;
   - left/right wall asymmetry;
   - floor slope;
   - horizontal curvature;
   - vertical curvature;
   - entrance width;
   - occasional chamber bulges;
   - local ceiling drops/rises.
9. Introduce irregular but bounded low-frequency cross-section distortion, not noisy jagged surfaces.
10. Cave chambers should feel excavated/formed inside terrain mass, not like standalone hollow cylinders.
11. Ensure all passages remain comfortably traversable by the accepted fish/camera.

C — BURY THE STRUCTURAL SHELL

12. The functional shell is collision/structural geometry, not the finished visual landmark.
13. Build/generated terrain mass around it so the shell appears to pass **through a hill/ridge/bank**.
14. Overlap/blend entrances heavily with:
    - generated ridge/terrain mass;
    - embedded FAF terrain accents;
    - large FAF rocks;
    - rocky-floor pieces;
    - rubble/detail pieces;
    - coral/vegetation where depth allows.
15. Target: from ordinary approach angles, only about **20–40% of the structural tunnel shell** should read as a distinct exposed object. The rest should disappear into connected geology.
16. Do not achieve this by simply covering the opening. Preserve clear entrance and route volume.
17. Entrance blending should extend several meters outward along the surrounding hillside so the geology transition is broad, not a one-ring prop halo.
18. Remove material/lighting contrast that makes structural geology read as one giant dark object. Reuse appropriate generated terrain/geology material language so the mass belongs to the world.

D — MULTIPLE GEOLOGY TYPES

19. Produce at minimum:
    - one broad/easy tunnel;
    - one tighter/curvier tunnel;
    - one cave chamber with at least one clear entrance and preferably a second exit where terrain permits;
    - one natural arch/overhang;
    - one canyon/ridge route.
20. Their location/shape must derive from seed-specific terrain, not the old fixed coordinates.

E — VALIDATION

Add focused checks for:

- selected geology sites have sufficient surrounding terrain mass;
- entrances/exits lie inside valid water-domain terrain;
- shell floor/wall/ceiling exist;
- clearance remains valid;
- structural shell is embedded/buried by surrounding terrain mass beyond a minimum envelope;
- entrance blend geometry does not block protected passage;
- same seed reproduces site selection and geometry;
- alternate seeds choose valid but different sites;
- no geology region is created as an isolated floating object in open water.

M2 should also re-run M1 basin/depth checks to ensure geology does not destroy shoreline/depth behavior.
