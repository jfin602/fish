IMPLEMENT

A — GENERATE THE BODY-OF-WATER FOOTPRINT FIRST

1. Replace the current approximately 35 m radial perimeter model as the master world shape.
2. Generate a deterministic irregular **2D water-domain / shoreline footprint** before computing terrain elevation.
3. The first serious target world should be approximately **250–350 m across**, with X/Z extents and local widths varying by seed.
4. The outline must contain broad natural irregularity:
   - lobes;
   - coves/inlets;
   - pinched/narrower regions;
   - wider bays;
   - asymmetric extensions;
   - uneven curvature.
5. It must not read as:
   - a square;
   - a perfect circle;
   - a slightly noisy circle;
   - a regular starburst.
6. Use low-frequency deterministic shape variation. Favor a small number of broad features over high-frequency coastline noise.
7. Provide a query such as distance-inside-domain / signed-distance-to-shore or equivalent. This becomes the authoritative input for depth generation.

B — GENERATE DEPTH INWARD FROM THE SHORELINE

8. Stop making radial distance from world origin the primary depth driver.
9. Compute an approximate **distance-to-shore field** over the generated water domain.
10. Convert that field into a broad water-depth profile so the exterior is naturally shallow and the interior trends deeper.
11. Initial target depth bands:
    - shoreline shallows: **0.5–2 m water depth**;
    - shallow reef: **2–6 m**;
    - middle water: **6–15 m**;
    - deep basin: **15–30 m**;
    - selected trenches/deep pockets: **30–45 m**.
12. These are art/gameplay targets, not exact physical simulation. The output must contain substantial usable area in multiple bands.
13. The deepest useful area should generally occur somewhere in the broad central portion of the body, but it must not be forced to the exact geometric center.
14. Keep enough genuinely shallow water near parts of the shoreline that the surface is visually close and future vegetation can become dense.

C — ADD MACRO TERRAIN AFTER THE DEPTH FIELD

15. Once the baseline shore-derived depth exists, add large terrain structure:
    - ridges;
    - channels;
    - shelves;
    - deep holes;
    - sandy flats;
    - rocky slopes;
    - secondary depressions.
16. These features may distort local depth, but they must not destroy the high-level shallow-edge -> deeper-interior relationship.
17. Avoid giant vertical bank walls. Shoreline approaches should usually transition through broad shallow shelves/slopes, with some steeper geological sections allowed as local features.
18. Keep the natural perimeter itself as terrain. Fail-safe containment may remain well outside/behind it, but should not be visible or reachable in ordinary play.

D — SCALE THE SUPPORT SYSTEMS

19. Update world/grid/domain constants, analysis extents, baked metadata and any terrain sampling assumptions that still hard-code the old ~80–100 m world.

    - environmental analysis grid;
    - route-planner search bounds;
    - water-surface coverage;
    - camera/depth reference metadata;
